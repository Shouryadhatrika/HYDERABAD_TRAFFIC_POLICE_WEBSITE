🪖 Helmet Safety Guidelines

Two-wheelers account for over 60% of road fatalities in Hyderabad. A certified helmet is the single most effective safety measure a rider can take — it reduces the risk of fatal head injury by up to 42%.

🏍️ Mandatory for all riders ISI / BIS Certified only Rider + Pillion
Why Helmet Safety Matters

Every year, hundreds of lives are lost on Hyderabad's roads due to head injuries — injuries that a properly worn helmet can prevent. The brain is the most vital and vulnerable organ in a collision. Unlike broken bones or lacerations, traumatic brain injury is often irreversible. A certified helmet acts as the last line of defence between the rider and the road, absorbing and distributing the energy of impact before it reaches the skull.

Hyderabad Traffic Police runs a year-round Helmet Compulsory Drive to ensure every two-wheeler rider — including pillion passengers — wears an ISI-certified full-face or open-face helmet. Officers conduct spot checks at major junctions, penalise violators, and actively distribute awareness material. Our officers have also been seen strapping helmets on children during community drives, because protection starts young and safety is everyone's responsibility.

Campaign in Action

Helmet Safety Rules

A good helmet must meet all of the following standards. Never compromise on any of these.

Should not impair hearing or obstruct vision
Should be lightweight — not add strain to the neck
Should not cause fatigue during extended wear
Should not cause skin irritation or allergic reactions
Should not increase the risk of neck injury on impact
Must carry ISI / BIS certification mark (IS:4151)
Must have thick foam padding — minimum 20 mm on all sides
Strap must always be fastened securely under the chin
⚠️
Full-face helmets are the safest type available and are strongly recommended. Half-face or open-face helmets offer reduced protection to the chin and face in a frontal collision. Never use decorative or uncertified helmets.
Helmet Components Explained
🛡️

The Outer Shell

The hard outer layer is engineered to do four critical things on impact:

  • Absorbs and disperses impact energy away from the skull
  • Distributes the force over a wider surface area
  • Allows the helmet to slide on the road surface, reducing rotational force on the brain
  • Protects the face, temples, and jaw from direct contact
🧠

The EPS Foam Liner

The expanded polystyrene (EPS) foam liner is the most critical safety layer inside the helmet. It works by compressing on impact, significantly increasing the stopping distance of the skull — this is what reduces brain injury. Once compressed from a serious impact, the foam cannot recover. Replace your helmet after any significant crash, even if it looks undamaged externally.

🔒

The Strapping System

A helmet that is not strapped is little more than a decoration. The chin strap must always be fastened snugly — you should be able to fit only one or two fingers between the strap and your chin. A loose strap dramatically reduces protection, as the helmet can fly off in a crash precisely when you need it most. Check strap condition before every ride.

Helmet Rules for Children

Children are especially vulnerable on two-wheelers. Know the rules before putting your child on a bike.

Age Group Helmet Required Additional Safety Speed Limit Remarks
Below 9 Months ⚠️ Not Defined — — Infants should not be carried on two-wheelers. Follow general safety precautions.
9 Months – 4 Years ✅ Yes Helmet + Safety Harness Max 40 km/h Child must wear an age-appropriate certified helmet and be secured with a harness.
Above 4 Years ✅ Mandatory Helmet Only Standard limits Same rules as adult pillion passengers. ISI-certified helmet compulsory.
🚦
Safety First — Wear a Helmet Always
Helmet use is not optional — it is the law under the Motor Vehicles Act. Riding without a helmet attracts a fine and can result in licence suspension. More importantly, it puts your life at risk. Hyderabad Traffic Police urges every rider to wear a certified helmet every single ride, every single time — no exceptions.
