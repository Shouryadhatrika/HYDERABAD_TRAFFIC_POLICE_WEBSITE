Traffic Marshal Scheme

Hyderabad Traffic Police's PPP initiative deploying trained marshals and patrol bikes to improve traffic flow and road safety across the city.

The Hyderabad Traffic Police launched the Traffic Marshal Scheme on August 21, 2025, deploying trained marshals and patrol bikes to improve traffic flow and road safety under a Public-Private Partnership (PPP) model. The initiative is spearheaded by the Hyderabad City Security Council (HCSC) to improve coordination for better city safety.

Scheme Overview
Structure & Personnel

Structure & Partnership:
The scheme operates as a Public-Private Partnership (PPP) wherein private firms, hospitals, and schools sponsor the marshals' salaries — making the program self-sustaining without government budget burden.

Personnel & Recruitment:
100–125 traffic marshals have been trained for 10–15 days at the Traffic Training Institute (TTI) in Goshamahal. The program has plans to scale up to 500 personnel across the city.

Uniform & Shifts:
Marshals wear a distinctive white uniform and work 8-hour shifts, focusing on traffic regulation and congestion control at key junctions and busy corridors.

Objectives & Impact

Primary Objective:
Improve traffic flow, reduce congestion, and enhance road safety at key locations across Hyderabad — especially during peak hours.

Measured Impact:
The scheme aims to deliver a 5–10% improvement in overall traffic efficiency at deployed junctions, reducing average commute delays for citizens.

  • Reduce junction congestion during peak hours
  • Support regular traffic police personnel
  • Faster incident response and crowd control
  • Promote road discipline among motorists
Patrol Bike Equipment

Each patrol bike deployed under the scheme is equipped with modern surveillance and enforcement technology to maximize effectiveness on the ground.

Body Cameras

Live recording of interactions and violations for accountability and evidence.

PA Systems

Public address speakers to guide motorists and manage crowd movement at junctions.

Dashboard Cameras

Forward-facing cameras to capture road conditions, violations, and incidents.

Towing Equipment

On-the-spot capability to address illegally parked or abandoned vehicles swiftly.

How the Scheme Works
1

PPP Sponsorship Secured

Private firms, hospitals, and schools partner with the Hyderabad City Security Council (HCSC) to sponsor marshal salaries. This ensures the scheme is financially sustainable without drawing from public funds.

2

Recruitment & Training at TTI

Candidates are recruited and undergo a structured 10–15 day training program at the Traffic Training Institute (TTI), Goshamahal. Training covers traffic regulation, hand signals, emergency response, and equipment operation.

3

Deployment at Key Locations

Trained marshals in white uniforms are deployed in 8-hour shifts at high-congestion junctions, busy corridors, school zones, and hospital areas across Hyderabad to actively manage traffic flow.

4

Patrol Bikes on Active Duty

Equipped patrol bikes provide rapid response capability — marshals can quickly reach incidents, manage violations, assist with towing of illegally parked vehicles, and broadcast instructions via PA systems.

5

Performance Monitoring & Scale-Up

Traffic efficiency improvements are continuously measured. With an initial batch of 100–125 marshals delivering a 5–10% efficiency boost, the program is on track to expand to 500 marshals city-wide.

PPP Sponsoring Partners

The Traffic Marshal Scheme is funded entirely through private sector partnerships, allowing Hyderabad Traffic Police to expand its workforce without additional budgetary allocations.

Private Firms

Corporate organizations sponsoring marshal salaries as part of their CSR and road safety commitments.

Hospitals

Healthcare institutions near busy roads sponsoring marshals to improve access for emergency vehicles and patients.

Schools

Educational institutions sponsoring marshals to manage traffic during peak school hours and protect student safety.

HCSC Coordination

Hyderabad City Security Council oversees the scheme, coordinating between sponsors, police, and marshal teams.

Hyderabad City Security Council (HCSC)

The Traffic Marshal Scheme is spearheaded by the HCSC, which coordinates multi-stakeholder partnerships between the traffic police, private sector, and civic institutions to improve coordination for better city safety and smarter urban traffic management.
